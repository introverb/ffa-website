Welcome to OURS

Tonight we gather around one crucial conviction: the future is something we make happen, not something that we let happen to us. We can imagine, discover, and build the world we want to live in. Everyone in this room, on these walls, and at the lectern is here because they're already doing exactly that: turning an optimistic, realistic vision of what's possible into something real.

Wander the work, watch the Visions of the Future videos, stay for the provocations, and most importantly, remember that the future is OURS.

— Olli Payne
Executive Director, FFA

Speakers
Tamara Winter
Economist · Commissioning editor, Stripe Press
Tamara Winter is the commissioning editor of Stripe Press, where she publishes the ideas she believes can help build a better future. Born in Nigeria and raised in Dallas, she traces her work to an early question about why some places grow and others stall, one that carried her through economics at SMU and roles at the Charter Cities Institute and the Mercatus Center. She treats a book as a long bet: a durable conversation between generations, and a vote for the arguments worth keeping.

Speakers
Erika Alden DeBenedictis
Physicist & biological engineer · CEO, Pioneer Labs
Erika is a physicist and biological engineer interested in solving big problems using evolution. She studied at Caltech (undergrad, computer science) and MIT (grad school, biological engineering). She led an academic lab at the Francis Crick Institute in London, where she invented robotics accelerated evolution methods. She is now the founder and CEO of Pioneer Labs, a Focused Research Organization (FRO) engineering life to make Mars more habitable.

Artist
RERO
A New City Will Be Built · burnt wood & metal · 67×49 in

RERO’s work is built upon a unique visual grammar: the use of the Verdana font (a symbol of the digital era), systematically crossed out with a horizontal line. Driven by punchlines and aphorisms, the betrayal of images, and semantic wordplay, RERO’s work lies at the crossroads of urban practices, land art, and conceptual gestures.

The dark, textured medium of burnt wood materializes the end of a model: that of rigid structures inherited from a saturated present. Yet, it is from the debris of this old world consumed by fire that the light of the letters emerges. By ending his phrase with an ellipsis, the artist refuses to close the narrative. The strikethrough creates a salutary void: it opens a breach and a space of indeterminacy, directly inviting visitors to reclaim their agency to collectively reimagine the contours of tomorrow's city.

“In Rero's work, art is an alarm system attempting to awaken the atrophied muscle of collective sensitivity,” wrote the great art critic Achille Bonito Oliva.

Sculptor · installation
Anyanwu
PYRAMID · 12×12×12 in · edition 1/5 · interactive

Anyanwu is the artistic practice of Nigerian artist, filmmaker, and world-builder Somto Ajuluchukwu.

At the heart of Anyanwu’s work is a central question: what might Africa’s societies, symbols, institutions, and systems of power have become if their evolution had not been interrupted by colonialism? His practice does not approach the past as nostalgia, but as a living source of possibility — a foundation from which new futures can be imagined, designed, and built.

For OURS, Anyanwu presents the Education sculpture from Systems of Power, a body of work that reimagines nation-building through six interdependent pillars: Housing, Health, Education, Environment, Infrastructure, and Economy. Each sculpture is conceived as both an architectural object and a philosophical symbol, asking how societies survive, evolve, and serve the people within them.

The Education sculpture reflects knowledge as one of the most vital infrastructures of any civilization. Drawing from the form of the pyramid as a symbol of ascent, accumulation, and structured wisdom, the work imagines education not simply as schooling, but as a system of memory, imagination, cultural continuity, and collective empowerment.

Through Systems of Power, Anyanwu invites viewers to see Africa not only as a place of history, but as a place of unfinished futures — futures that can still be reclaimed, redesigned, and made ours.

Giorgia Lupi · Modelli Circolari · 02 Blue
Gouache, acrylic, ink & threads on paper · 14×18 in
Blue is part of a series called Modelli Circolari, and they stem from my obsession with the things I am eager to understand or fascinated by. Each piece has an inner focus circle that hosts symbols, connections, diagrams and words.

02 Blue maps the hidden structure of personal identity and its evolution: how a self is built, layered, and revised over time. What I cannot yet express in language I can lay down on paper and keep looking at.

Seungjun Na· printed collage
Printed collage · 36×48 in · self-standing
Commissioned by Medici Magazine
This piece is meant to evoke the feeling of discovering another small world hidden within a secluded pond surrounded by lush plants. On a lotus leaf floating in the pond, there is a miniature world where children are building structures made of LEGO bricks, representing technology built upon art and science. The lotus leaf also has a sail. It symbolizes the hope that everyone on it is moving together toward the same destination with a shared purpose.

I chose children as the main characters because I believe that people who still retain a childlike sense of curiosity and innocence are often the ones capable of imagining groundbreaking changes and truly original ideas. I look forward to a future where such ideas open the door to entirely new worlds.

Sue Ellen Zhang · The Hummingbird
Oil on canvas · 30×40 in
The Hummingbird draws from the Futurist movement's fascination with speed, motion, and technological progress. I wanted to translate that sense of energy through softer, organic forms inspired by the natural world.

The painting imagines a future in which technology and nature are not opposing forces, but systems capable of evolving together. Its movement suggests both possibility and the responsibility to shape progress with care.

Gallery
Sev Gedra · O Quam Cito
Garment · on loan
O Quam Cito is a life-sized figure wrapped in inherited household linen encrusted in woven amber that cascades from the face and pools at the feet. The linen is the most intimate material I could find: domestic, inherited, private.
The figure is faceless because legends have no single author. These stories are woven, strand by strand, hand to hand, each retelling adding something, the origin receding. To think about the future is to think about what survives us. The amber carries what we valued. The gaps in the weave hold space for future generations.

From the Foundation
Art, on-chain
Every work on this wall lives on a public blockchain. For artists, that ledger is practical, not hype: it records authorship permanently, pays royalties automatically on every resale, and lets collectors anywhere support a career directly — no gatekeepers, no lost provenance.
The NFT is not the point; the guarantees are. To embrace this ledger is to give a work permanence, a livelihood, and a public a way to hold it — reasons enough for any artist to consider it. Scan any placard on the wall to collect; the artist is paid the moment you do.
